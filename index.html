    Is a currency too strong or too weak? The Big Mac index, July 2026

    The question it answers: does a Big Mac cost more or less in each country than in the US, once the price is converted to dollars? A big gap hints that a currency looks overvalued (+) or undervalued (−).

    Raw: at market exchange ratesGDP-adjustedComparison country (raw)Dark line = fair value (the US)

    Move your cursor or finger across the chart, or use the slider, to read each date. Valuation is measured against the US dollar.

    Press Play under the chart, or drag the slider, to watch the ranking change from 2000 to 2026. Scroll inside the frame to see all of it. Open it in its own tab.

    Click the chart to zoom in.

    What to noticeA Big Mac costs 45% more in Switzerland than in the US and 62% less in Indonesia. Adjusting for income barely changes the typical gap (27% raw, 25% adjusted).
    Why this chartIt was my first data visualisation for my Python course, and a good way to practise ranking, labelling and checking a chart against its data.
    What I addedA “change between two dates” view that shows how far each country moved. For example, Turkey rose by about 69 percentage points between July 2020 and July 2026.

    Copy of a course chart. A copy of the MSIN0167 course target chart, built as a class exercise with AI help. It is inspired by The Economist’s Big Mac index and is not a chart by The Economist.
